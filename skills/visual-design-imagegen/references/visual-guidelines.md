# Visual Guidelines

## Direction

Derive the visual direction from the subject, audience, and references. In the absence of a stated style, favor a contemporary, clear, restrained treatment with an intentional grid, readable hierarchy, and enough contrast for the intended viewing distance.

Translate adjectives into observable choices. For example, “calm and trustworthy” can mean lower saturation, stable alignment, restrained depth, and limited accent colors; “energetic” can mean stronger contrast, more directional rhythm, and bolder type scale without sacrificing legibility.

## Composition

- Establish one dominant reading path and one clear visual focus.
- Use spacing, alignment, scale, color, and grouping to express hierarchy before adding decoration.
- Keep the number of container styles, radii, shadows, and accent colors small enough to form a coherent system.
- Match information density to viewing distance: denser for close desktop use, simpler for rooms, mobile feeds, and large displays.
- Use common UI and chart forms when they help the audience understand the concept quickly.

## Text and data

- Reserve high-fidelity rendering for the title, primary labels, required facts, and user-supplied values.
- Shorten or visually de-emphasize secondary copy when exact rendering is not required.
- Use consistent number formatting, units, color semantics, and status labels.
- Avoid presenting invented content with the visual authority of a confirmed fact.

## Medium

- Render the requested artifact itself as a complete, front-facing canvas.
- Add device frames, browser chrome, physical environments, perspective, or photographic staging only when they are part of the brief.
- Keep generated artifacts free of accidental watermarks, signatures, source logos, and reference-image content.

## Quality check

At intended viewing size, verify the focal point, major regions, exact labels, supplied numbers, contrast, alignment, and source-content exclusions. Correct material failures before delivery.
